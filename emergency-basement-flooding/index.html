In Plain English

What To Do Right Now — and What We Do When We Arrive

First, stay safe. Do not enter standing water if there may be contact with electrical equipment. Call emergency services or the utility company if there is an immediate electrical danger. No basement is worth an electrical injury.

If it is safe to act: shut off water at the main valve if the flood is from a burst pipe, move what you can to higher ground, and keep children and pets away from the water. Take photos for your insurance company — but only from safe, dry ground.

Every hour matters in a flood. Drywall wicks water upward, flooring delaminates, and damp materials can begin developing mold within a day or two. Fast water removal is the difference between drying out and tearing out.

When you call, we respond: emergency water extraction, pump-out of standing water, emergency sump pump replacement if yours failed, and identification of the entry point so we can stop active inflow. Once the water is out, we inspect and give you a clear plan — and a written estimate — for making sure this does not happen again.

Is This You?

While You Wait for Help

  • Keep everyone away from water near outlets, cords or appliances
  • Shut off the main water valve if a burst pipe is the source
  • Move valuables and electronics up and out of the water's path
  • Do not run a wet/dry vacuum while standing in water
  • Photograph the damage from safe, dry ground
  • Open no electrical panels — leave that to the utility or an electrician
