In Plain English

Why Cleaning Method Matters More Than Cleaning Power

Brick and stone both have a protective outer surface. On brick it is the hard fired face; on stone it is the dressed surface that has weathered into a stable skin. Once that layer is destroyed, the soft, absorbent material underneath is exposed permanently — and it will soak up water and deteriorate far faster than before.

That is what makes aggressive cleaning so damaging. Sandblasting and high-pressure washing strip the protective face off masonry in minutes. Countless Chicago buildings were permanently ruined this way in past decades, and the damage cannot be undone.

Different stains also need different chemistry. Efflorescence is mineral salt carried out by moisture. Soot is carbon-based. Organic growth is biological. Rust comes from corroding metal. Using the wrong product wastes money and can set the stain permanently.

We identify what we are removing, test on a discreet area first, and use the lowest effective method — controlled low-pressure water, appropriate specialist detergents, or poultices for stubborn staining. You see the test result before we clean the whole building.
