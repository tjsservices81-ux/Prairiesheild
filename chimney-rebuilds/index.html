Warning Signs

Signs a Chimney Needs Rebuilding

If any of these look familiar, it is worth a free inspection — small problems are the cheap ones.

Widespread crumbling mortar throughout the stack
Many bricks spalled, cracked or missing
The stack visibly leaning or twisted
Masonry separated at the roofline
Repairs that have failed more than once
Bricks falling from the chimney regularly
In Plain English

Partial vs. Full Rebuild — and How We Decide

Rebuilding is not the first answer, it is the honest answer when repair will not hold. A chimney with sound lower masonry but a deteriorated top section is a candidate for a partial rebuild — taking it down to solid material and rebuilding above the roofline.

A full rebuild becomes necessary when deterioration runs the whole height, when the stack has moved significantly, or when the base masonry is crushing. Rebuilding a stack whose footing is still settling would simply produce a new chimney with the same old problem — so we assess the footing first.

Rebuilding is an opportunity to correct original defects. Many older Chicago chimneys were built without proper flashing details, adequate crown overhang or correct flue support. A rebuild puts those details right.

We rebuild using brick matched to your home, appropriate mortar, a properly formed crown with drip edge and overhang, and correctly integrated flashing, so the new stack sheds water instead of soaking it up.

Frequently Asked Questions

A partial rebuild takes the chimney down to sound masonry — usually at or just below the roofline — and rebuilds from there. A full rebuild goes to the base or footing. We recommend the smallest rebuild that will genuinely last, and explain exactly where the sound material starts.

We assess mortar condition, brick integrity, plumb and the footing. Isolated damage with sound surrounding masonry means repair. Widespread mortar failure, many failed bricks, or significant movement means rebuilding is the repair that actually holds.

We select brick matched in size, color and texture, and mortar matched to your existing walls. On older homes reclaimed brick often gives the best blend. You approve the material before we build.

Yes — and this is where being a foundation contractor matters. If the footing under your chimney is settling, rebuilding the stack alone would repeat the failure. We can stabilize or underpin the base as part of the project.

Partial rebuilds are commonly two to four days; full rebuilds take longer and depend on height, access and weather. Masonry needs suitable temperatures to cure, so winter scheduling is planned carefully.
