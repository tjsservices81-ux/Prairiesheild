## Part 2 — Before and after (30 pairs = 60 photos)

Each of the 30 service pages needs **one** before/after pair. Extra pairs
are shown if they exist, but one is the target.

### The rule that matters

A before/after pair only works if it is genuinely the same spot:

- **Same position.** Stand in the same place. Mark it — a chalk X, a
  paving slab, anything.
- **Same angle and height.** Chest height both times is fine, as long as
  it is the same both times.
- **Same framing.** Same amount of wall in shot. Do not zoom in on the
  "after" to make it look better.
- **Similar light.** Same time of day if you can. An "after" in bright
  sun against a "before" in shade looks like a trick.
- **Take the before first, always.** Before anyone touches anything, and
  before the scaffold goes up. This is the one that gets forgotten.

Easiest way: take the before, then keep that photo open on the phone and
line the after up against it.

## How to shoot them

- **Phone is fine.** A modern phone beats a bad camera. Clean the lens.
- **Landscape**, always. The site is built wide — a portrait photo gets
  cropped and loses the top and bottom.
- **Biggest size the phone allows.** They get compressed for the web
  later; they cannot be made bigger.
- **Overcast is best.** Harsh midday sun makes hard black shadows on
  brick and hides the detail.
- **No people's faces** without asking them first. No house numbers, no
  street signs, no number plates — customers do not want their address
  online.
- **Tidy the shot.** Move the wheelbarrow, coil the hose, pick up the
  buckets. It takes ten seconds and it changes how professional the work
  looks.
- **Name the file on the spot** if possible, or send them in batches per
  job with the service written in the message. Untitled batches of 200
  photos are impossible to sort later.

## Where to start

This order gets the site looking real fastest:

**Stage 1 — 6 photos.** The homepage hero, the team photo, and finished
photos of the four services on the homepage: Foundation Repair,
Residential Masonry, Basement Waterproofing, Chimney Repair.

**Stage 2 — 30 photos.** One finished photo for every service page, so
no page repeats another page's picture.

**Stage 3 — ongoing.** One before/after pair per service. Take the before
on every job as a habit, even the small ones — roughly one job per service
and this is finished.
