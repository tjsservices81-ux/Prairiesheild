In Plain English

Why Sump Pumps Fail — and How to Be Ready

A sump pump collects the groundwater that gathers under and around your foundation and pushes it safely away from the house. In much of Chicagoland, where water tables rise fast during storms, the pump is not an accessory — it is the last line of defense.

Pumps fail for predictable reasons: worn motors, stuck float switches, clogged intakes, undersized capacity and simple old age. The most common failure of all has nothing to do with the pump — it is a power outage. The same storm that fills your sump pit is the one most likely to knock out your electricity.

A failed pump turns a normal rainstorm into a flooded basement in hours. If your pump is old, loud, cycling oddly or has ever been overwhelmed, it is telling you it cannot be trusted with the next big storm.

Prairie Shield sizes the right pump for your water volume, installs it with proper check valves and discharge routing, and adds a battery backup system that takes over automatically when the power dies or the primary pump fails. We can also service pits, replace aging pumps and add high-water alarms.
