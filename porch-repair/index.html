In Plain English

Why Chicago Porches Fail

Most porches sit on shallow footings or on soil that was disturbed and backfilled when the house was built. That backfill was never compacted like undisturbed ground, so as it settles over the years the porch sinks and tilts with it, opening a gap where it meets the house.

Porches also take direct weather abuse: standing water, de-icing salt and dozens of freeze-thaw cycles every winter. Salt accelerates surface scaling on concrete, and water sitting in cracks pries them wider each season. Brick porches add failing joints and loose units to the list.

A tilted porch causes a second problem most homeowners miss: it drains toward the house instead of away, funneling water against your foundation and door sill — which is exactly how basement water problems start.

We diagnose whether the issue is settlement, surface deterioration or both, then repair: rebuilding masonry, repointing joints, resurfacing or replacing concrete, correcting pitch so water drains away, and stabilizing settled structures — including with piers where a porch is worth saving.
